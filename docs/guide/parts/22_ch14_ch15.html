Flow in time: pathlines

A streamline is the path along a field frozen at one moment. A pathline is the track of a particle carried by a field that changes with time - it is what a drop of dye would show. When the flow is unsteady the two differ, and pathlines are the ones that match reality. ModelViewer draws pathlines from a time-dependent formula or from a CSV time series, colours them by time, and can animate them.

Pathlines from a CSV time series

The table is a velocity field sampled on a complete regular grid for each of a series of times. Each row holds a time, a position and a velocity:

t,x,y,u,v,w
0,0,0,-0.000000,0.000000,0
0,0,0.25,-0.000000,0.222144,0
...
  • Choose Data source CSV time series (pathlines) and open or paste the table. Time column, X, Y, an optional Z and U, V, W are guessed from the header names (t or time, x, u ...) and can be changed. Without a Z column the field is planar.
  • The status line reports either N rows form a complete grid and how many seeds and segments result, or why the table cannot be used: a missing row (it says how many rows are needed), a non-numeric cell (it names the row), a column left out.
  • How it is traced. Seeds are released along Y at the middle of the X range (in the middle z plane for a 3-D table) and carried through the field with a fourth-order Runge-Kutta integration, over time steps equal steps from the first to the last time in the table. The field is interpolated linearly in space and in time. A trail ends where its particle leaves the grid. The row order of the file does not matter.
  • The trails are lines coloured by time, so the legend runs from the start to the end time, and they take the usual colour map, bands, range and line-width controls.
SampleWhat it is
pathlines_double_gyre.csvThe classic unsteady double gyre on x 0..2, y 0..1: two counter-rotating cells whose dividing line oscillates with a period of 10 s, sampled at 21 times (945 rows). A planar field.
pathlines_rising_vortex_3d.csvA 3-D field (4,212 rows with a Z column): a rotation about the Z axis whose rate pulses in time plus an oscillating vertical velocity, so the trails climb as they circle.
pathlines_openfoam_cavity.csvThe cell-centre velocity of the OpenFOAM cavity sample (times 0 to 2 s) on a plane just above its top face, made to be played together with that result (chapter 18).

Pathlines from a formula

With Formula pathlines (time-dependent) the field is three expressions u(x,y,z,t), v(x,y,z,t), w(x,y,z,t) in position and time; Y range / samples sets the seeds along Y at the middle of the X range (at z = 0), and T range / steps the time interval and the number of integration steps. A trail ends where its particle leaves the x / y range.

PresetBehaviour
Pulsating VortexA rigid rotation whose rate pulses in time: concentric rings; the colour (time) bands bunch up where the particle slows and stretch where it speeds up.
Double GyreTwo counter-rotating cells whose dividing line oscillates; neighbouring trails fold and stretch.
Travelling WaveA steady stream deflected sideways by a wave travelling in x: the trails drift forward and wiggle.
Oscillating UpdraftA vortex with a vertical velocity that oscillates: the rings climb and sink, so the trails draw helices in Z.
Caution

In this source t is time. In Formula streamlines the field is steady (no time), and in Parametric curve t is merely the curve parameter. If the end of the time range is before its start, Build Plot is disabled with a message.

Animating pathlines

Select the plot in Active plot and tick Animate pathlines (timeline) in the 3D Plot tab. A timeline appears at the top of the viewport - the same bar as for simulation results, with play/pause, step, stop, a slider, the current time, Loop and a speed choice. The trails are drawn only up to the current time, with a dot at the head of each, coloured by the current time. Switching the option on shows the whole plot until you press play; from the end, play starts over; unticking the option brings back the full trails and removes the bar. Hiding the plot in the scene tree removes the bar and pauses playback.

The animation is a viewing aid: it is not saved with the file and the head dots are not exported.

Styling, axes and scales

Everything about a built plot - colours, sizes, the axes - is in the 3D Plot tab of the dock (next to the Simulation Results tab). Choose the plot in Active plot; the controls act on it. The plot's entry in the scene tree has the visibility checkbox.

The axes box and the reference planes

Show axes box frames the plot with labelled axes, ticks and numbers; hiding it does not discard the layout. Planes adds the reference planes XY, XZ and YZ at the minimum of the other axes, with an Opacity. A flat axis (all values equal) is padded by a tenth of the largest other extent, so a plane does not turn into a needle.

Shared axes

All plots in a document are drawn on one set of axes - the same scales, ranges and labels - because that is what lets you compare them. Under Axes every axis has a label, an Axis scale, a range and a tick count. Changing one re-lays out all the plots together, whichever plot is selected; a plot built later joins the axes that are already there.

ScaleMeaning and limit
LinearThe default.
Log 10Powers of ten. Needs the data of every plot to be above zero on that axis. Setting it while a plot reaches zero is refused with a message naming the reason; building a plot that cannot be shown on an existing Log 10 axis returns that axis to Linear for all plots, with a status message.
SymLogLogarithmic for large values, linear around zero; works for any data including negative values. Voxel indices that start at 0 and vector fields with both signs are examples.
  • Lines, scatter, surfaces, bars, vector arrows and voxel volumes all follow the scale: the geometry is transformed, not just relabelled.
  • Vector arrows are re-aimed by the local stretch of the scale at their site and keep their length (it carries the magnitude) and colour; voxel grids are resampled onto a grid that is regular in the scaled space. Returning to Linear restores everything.
  • The hover readout of a surface still shows the plot's data values, not positions on the scaled axes.
