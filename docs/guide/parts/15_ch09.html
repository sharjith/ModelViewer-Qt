Saving, large results and limits

Saving a result in a session

A result is a viewing session like any other, so it is saved in the .mvf session file (File → Save). The point of saving is that the session reopens without the original solver file, with the field, range, colormap, units, deformation and timeline as you left them. When the document contains results, ModelViewer asks what to store, once per session:

What is stored

  • The snapshot is the boundary surface, not the volume: values are stored per surface vertex (not per solver node), compressed without loss, as 32-bit numbers. The dialog shows a size estimate before anything is written.
  • The steps (with their times or labels), the fields with their units and quantities, the all-solver-nodes range of each field and step (so the legend and the all-steps colour range are identical to the live result even though interior nodes are not stored), and the view state: field, component, range, colormap, bands, step, deformation and markers.
  • The derived stress fields are not stored: they are recomputed from the stress tensor on loading.
  • At most 100 time steps are stored per result, evenly spaced; the dialog tells you if that applies.
  • The path of the source file is kept as information only; the session never needs it.

Sections, iso-surfaces and streamlines in a saved session

Without the volume, only the visible surface is stored, so the cut faces, iso-surfaces and streamlines on display are saved as they were and come back frozen: they no longer follow a moved clipping plane or another time step, and the panel says so. Tick Also store the volume to keep them live: the nodes, cells and chosen fields of the whole volume are stored too, the restored result is the full result, and everything - sections, iso-surfaces, streamlines, volume rendering - recomputes as before. The price is a much larger file, roughly in proportion to the volume; the dialog shows what the volume adds.

Reopening

  • An older version of ModelViewer opens such a session as a plain coloured mesh (the shown colours are stored with it) and ignores the rest.
  • If you edit the mesh of a stored result after loading - split, merge, repair - and save, the vertex count no longer matches the stored data; on the next load the result session is dropped with a message rather than showing wrong values. Moving, rotating or scaling the model is harmless.

Large results

Where the memory goes

Geometry is cheap; time steps are what grow. A result holds, for every field and every step, four bytes per value:

Mesh (hexahedra)GeometryOne step of a node scalar, a node vector and a cell scalar50 steps, all read at once
1 million cellsabout 50 MBabout 20 MBabout 1 GB
5 million cellsabout 250 MBabout 100 MBabout 5 GB
20 million cellsabout 1 GBabout 410 MBabout 20 GB

These are sizes of what is held in memory; Compare mode holds two results at once.

Lazy step loading

To keep large transient results usable, steps are loaded on demand. A result is read lazily when the data of its steps would need more than 256 MB and it has more than one step; the formats that support it are Exodus, CalculiX FRD, CGNS, VTKHDF (unstructured grids and poly data) and OpenFOAM cases. The other formats, and VTKHDF image data, are read in full; MED is not lazy yet.

  • Opening reads only the mesh and the field headers; the last four steps that were shown stay in memory, the least recently used going first. An OpenFOAM case reads one time directory per step. A CalculiX file is memory-mapped and indexed once.
  • A step that is not resident is read from the file when you move to it, so scrubbing a lazy result is slower than a resident one, and the file stays open (a CalculiX .frd is locked for writing while the result is loaded).
  • The range over all steps - the default colour range - reads every step once on first use; a busy cursor covers that first scan.
  • Saving a lazy result reads the whole file, because the snapshot stores every step it keeps.

Practical advice

  • Write the fields and steps you need from the solver; every extra step costs time and disk space, in the file and in a saved session.
  • Prefer binary, structured formats (VTKHDF, Exodus, CGNS) over ASCII .vtk or .frd for big transient results.
  • Opening takes time in two stages: reading the file, then extracting the boundary surface (a few seconds for five million cells; the extraction is done in parallel).
  • Close results you no longer look at, and avoid Compare with two huge results.

Limits and what is not supported

LimitDetails
No solving or editingModelViewer neither meshes nor solves, edits values, or creates difference (A minus B) fields.
Open formats onlyNo proprietary formats (Abaqus .odb, ANSYS .rst, Nastran OP2); export from those programs to VTK or another open format first.
Shells have no insideSections, iso-surfaces, streamlines and volume rendering need volume cells.
Overlays are not meshesCut faces, iso-surfaces, streamlines, arrows and ellipsoids cannot be selected, exported to glTF/OBJ, or drawn by the ray tracer. Results themselves are drawn flat in every rendering mode and are not ray traced.
Streamline fieldsNode vector fields only; concave polyhedra are excluded from tracing.
