About this guide

ModelViewer began as a viewer for 3D models. Two of its newer areas go beyond models: it shows the results of engineering simulations that were calculated elsewhere, and it plots your own data in 3D. This guide covers both in depth, and shows how they work together. The tutorial lessons (Help → Tutorial, lessons 19 to 25) walk through the same ground step by step; this guide is the reference you keep open next to the program.

What ModelViewer does and does not do

ModelViewer displays the results that a solver has written. It does not mesh, solve or run simulations, it does not edit results, and it has no scripting or expression language for post-processing. Its strengths are the ones a viewer needs: fast, correct colours; units; time; sections and probes; side-by-side comparison; and the presentation tools (measurement, annotation, PDF report, image export) that the rest of the program offers.

Who it is for

Engineers who read the output of structural, thermal or flow analyses (CalculiX, Code_Aster, Elmer, FreeCAD FEM, OpenFOAM, MOOSE and similar open solvers) and want to inspect, compare and present it; and anyone who has tables of numbers - measurements, simulation exports, mathematical functions - and wants to see them as surfaces, curves, points, bars, volumes or vector fields next to a 3D model.

Conventions

  • Menu paths use arrows: File → Open.... Names of controls, tabs and buttons are in bold, spelled as in the English interface.
  • Keys look like Ctrl+O. File names and column names are in monospace.
  • Boxes marked Note add background, Tip a shortcut or good habit, Caution something that surprises people or a limit of the program.
  • All sample files named here ship with ModelViewer in the sample-models folder (Simulation and Plot3D); Appendix A lists them. The pictures were taken with the English interface.
