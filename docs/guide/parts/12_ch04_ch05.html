The deformed shape and modal results

Showing the deformation

Structural results contain a displacement for every node. Tick Show deformed shape and the model is drawn displaced by that field instead of at rest. The displacement is applied as the model is drawn; the geometry in the file and the rest positions are not altered, so you can switch the option off again at any time.

  • Scale factor exaggerates the displacement. Real deflections are usually tiny compared with the model, so a factor well above 1 is normal. The Auto button picks a factor that makes the movement clearly visible.
  • The displacement is applied in the file's own length unit; the panel names the field that is used. A result without a displacement field cannot be shown deformed, and says so.
  • While the deformed shape is on, everything that follows the geometry follows the displaced mesh: the hover readout, the extrema markers, sections, iso-surfaces and streamlines.
Caution

The picture is exaggerated on purpose. When you judge how much a part really moves, read the scale factor, and use the hover readout or a chart for the true displacement values.

Modal results

A frequency analysis produces mode shapes, not displacements with a physical size: a mode only says how the structure moves relative to itself, and its amplitude is arbitrary. ModelViewer treats such results (steps labelled Mode 1, Mode 2 ... with their frequencies in hertz) specially:

  • Each mode is drawn with its largest displacement at a tenth of the model size, so every mode is visible without fiddling; the Scale factor scales that (a factor of 1 means a tenth of the model size).
  • The steps are the modes. The timeline steps through them and plays them one after another; the label shows the mode's frequency.
  • The x axis of a chart of a modal result is the frequency of each mode, which is what makes a frequency-response curve line up with the modes (chapter 7).
Note

Samples to try: FEM_box_modes.frd (six modes) and FEM_box_frequency_mode1.vtu (the first mode, as written by FreeCAD). FEM_box_static_stress.vtu and plate_with_hole.frd are good for the ordinary deformed shape; plate_with_hole.frd has four load steps.

Time: steps and the timeline

What a step is

The word step covers three different things, and ModelViewer treats them alike:

  • Load steps and increments of a structural analysis (for example 25 %, 50 %, 87.5 %, 100 % of a load);
  • Time steps of a transient thermal or flow analysis (an OpenFOAM case has one per time directory);
  • Modes of a frequency analysis (chapter 4).

A result with more than one step shows a timeline at the top of the viewport. A result with a single step has no timeline.

Colours over time

By default the colour range covers all steps (chapter 3), so a colour means the same value in every frame and you can watch a stress build up or heat flow in. Choose Automatic (this step) when you want each step to use its own full range - for instance to see the shape of a mode - and Custom to fix the scale yourself.

Charts and time

The timeline and the time charts of chapter 7 drive each other: an orange cursor in a chart marks the current step, and clicking or dragging in the chart moves the result to the nearest step. When a result and an animated 3D plot share one playback bar (chapter 18), the bar follows physical time.

Long transient results

A result with many steps and many cells holds a lot of data. Results whose step data would need more than 256 MB, and which have more than one step, are read lazily: only a few steps are in memory at a time and the others are read from the file when they are shown. Opening is fast and memory stays flat, at the price of a short read each time you move to a step that is not resident, and of the first scan for the all-steps colour range. Chapter 9 explains this and its consequences.
