Results and plots on one clock

A simulation result and a plot live in the same scene, so they can be shown together. When both change over time, one playback bar plays them in step. Which combination makes sense depends on whether the data shares coordinates and time.

Four situations

You haveSame coordinates?What is synchronised, and how it is shown
A transient flow or thermal result and pathlines of the same flow (for instance a CSV time series exported from the solver)YesPhysical time. One view, overlaid: the result's time equals the pathlines' time. Use All together on the playback bar (below).
A result and a chart of a point's history, maximum stress against time, a reaction force, or measured test dataNo (a curve is not in model space)Physical time, with a moving cursor on the chart and click-to-seek (chapter 7). The model and the chart are shown beside each other.
A modal analysis and a frequency-response curveNoFrequency. The chart's cursor sits at the current mode's frequency; clicking a peak selects the mode.
A load-step or parametric sweep and a response curveNoThe step's value against the chart's axis.
The rule behind it

Data is matched by physical value, not by progress. A result from 0 to 2 s and pathlines from 0 to 1.5 s line up at 1 s. Matching by progress (0 to 100 %) is only the fallback when the units are incompatible - a modal result is in hertz and pathlines are in seconds - and the playback bar then says by progress.

The playback bar for both

The timeline plays whichever item is chosen in the box at its left. The box lists every multi-step simulation result (Simulation: name), the animated pathline plot (Pathlines: name) and, when both can play, All together. With only one playable item the box is hidden. The bar follows what you work on: choosing a result in the Simulation Results tab selects it, and ticking Animate pathlines (timeline) selects that plot. Changing the selected item pauses the one that was playing. The legends of a result and a plot stack in one column, each headed with its owner's name.

Worked example: the cavity flow

  1. Open sample-models/Simulation/openfoam_cavity/cavity.foam and choose U with the Magnitude component.
  2. In the 3D Plot tab choose Add 3D Plot..., source CSV time series (pathlines), open sample-models/Plot3D/pathlines_openfoam_cavity.csv and map Time = t, X = x, Y = y, Z = z, U = u, V = v, W = w. This file holds the cell-centre velocity of the same case on a plane just above its top face, so the two share coordinates and real time. Build the plot; hide its axes box if it clutters the view.
  3. Tick Animate pathlines (timeline), choose All together in the playback box and press play. The trails grow while the field steps from 0 to 2 s; the bar reads the time and the step, for example t = 1 – step 3 of 5.
Caution

A modal result such as FEM_box_modes.frd has steps in hertz and cannot share a time axis with pathlines in seconds; All together then plays them by progress, and says so.

Charts as the second half

For the other three situations the chart does the work: plot a point's history (right-click → Plot Over Time Here), add your measured curve from a CSV, and play. The cursor travels along both curves; click in the chart to jump in time. For a modal result add a frequency-response CSV and click a peak to jump to that mode. Chapter 7 has the details and the sample files.

Worked workflows

Recipes that combine the chapters. Each lists the sample files to try it with.

Review a structural analysis

  1. Open the result (plate_with_hole.frd), look at the info line, and set the units under Values are in: this file is in mm and MPa.
  2. Colour by von Mises, keep Automatic (all steps), tick Mark minimum and maximum.
  3. Switch on Show deformed shape with Auto scale, and play the four load steps.
  4. Turn on Show stress ellipsoids (count 1500+) to see the stress concentration at the hole.
  5. Use Plot Over Line from the edge of the hole to the plate edge to read how the stress falls off, and save the chart image.
  6. Place two annotations (Tools → Annotate...) at the maximum, capture views in the Cameras panel and export the report (Tools → Export Report...).

A thermal transient against measurements

  1. Open FEM_box_thermal_transient.frd; the file states no temperature unit, so choose one under Values are in.
  2. Right-click the middle of the far face → Plot Over Time Here.
  3. In the chart, right-click → Add curve from CSV... → thermal_probe_test.csv. Play the timeline and compare the curves.
  4. Add a second point (Add a point from the model) near the heated face to see the heat arrive earlier. Save the chart as an image.

A flow with its particle tracks

Follow the cavity example of chapter 18. For a steady 3-D flow without a time series, use streamlines of the result instead: duct.cgns with Show streamlines (Velocity), Seed on the Clipping Plane and a clipping plane across the duct.

Modes and a frequency response

  1. Open FEM_box_modes.frd, switch on the deformed shape, and step through the six modes.
  2. Right-click a point → Plot Over Time Here: the x axis is the mode frequency.
  3. Add FEM_box_modes_frf.csv and click a peak: the result jumps to the corresponding mode.

Two design variants

  1. Open the first result, add the second with Add Result to This Document....
  2. Compare side by side with Same colour range for both and Link the cameras (ibeam_cantilever.frd against ibeam_torsion.frd).
  3. Exit compare and chart the same point of each result; export both charts as CSV and load one into the other's chart to see the two curves together.

Measured data next to a CAD model

  1. Open the CAD part, then add a scatter plot of your measurements (columns x, y, z and a deviation as the colour value); both are in the same coordinates if the table uses the model's units.
  2. Set the axes to match the model, add notes at points of interest, and hide the axes box if you only want the points.
  3. For a picture of the specimen, add an Image on a plane at the right place.
Tip

Save the document as .mvf at each stage worth keeping (chapter 9 explains what a saved result contains): a session with results, plots, annotations and captured views is the best single record of an analysis review.
