Looking inside a result

The surface of a solid shows only half the story: the highest stress is often inside a section, the hottest region under the skin, the fastest flow in the middle of a duct. ModelViewer offers six ways to look in, from the simplest (a cut) to the most specialised (stress tensors as glyphs). The first four need volume cells (tetrahedra, hexahedra, wedges, pyramids, polyhedra); on a shell result the panel states that there is nothing to cut or trace. All six are overlays: they are drawn with the result but are not scene meshes, so they cannot be selected, exported or drawn by the ray tracer.

Sections: the cut coloured by the field

The Clipping Planes (the Section View button of the View toolbar; see the tutorial lesson 12) cut a result like any model. Tick Colour the Clipping Plane cut with the field and the cut face shows the shown field - with the colours of the legend - instead of an empty hollow. The coloured cut follows the plane as you move it.

  • It works with a node field (smooth) and with a cell field (flat per cell).
  • With several planes switched on, a plane's cut is kept only where every other plane has removed the material, so the cuts always lie on the boundary of what is left.
  • Only the axis-aligned planes take part, not the box mode and not custom planes.
  • The cut is computed on the volume cells, and concave polyhedra are supported.

Iso-surfaces

An iso-surface is the surface on which a field has one constant value - a shell of equal temperature, a layer of equal stress. Show iso-surfaces draws a set of them for the Iso-surface field (a node scalar, or the magnitude of a node vector), at Iso-surface levels numbering 1 to 20, spaced evenly strictly inside the range of the field at the current step, each in its own colour. A constant field has no iso-surface; the info line says so.

  • Iso-surfaces lie inside the model, so they are seen through a cut. Switch on a Clipping Plane: the section cap is kept open while iso-surfaces are shown, because an opaque cap would hide them. The opaque coloured cut does hide what is behind it; switch that option off to see the iso-surfaces.
  • They are recomputed when the step or the field changes.

Streamlines

Streamlines follow a velocity field: each line is the path a massless particle would take if the flow were frozen at the current step. Show streamlines uses a node vector field (Streamline field); Streamline seeds (1 to 500) is the number of start points.

  • Seeding. By default the seeds are random points of the volume - the same ones every time, so animation frames stay comparable. Seed on the Clipping Plane starts the lines from the cut faces of the enabled planes instead, weighted by area, which shows exactly where the flow goes from a cross-section. A seed outside the mesh, or where the field is zero, gives no line.
  • Tracing. Lines are integrated with a fourth-order Runge-Kutta method in both directions from a seed until they leave the mesh, the speed falls to about a millionth of the largest, or a limit is reached (2000 steps, or four model diagonals, per direction). The field is interpolated exactly inside the cell it passes through.
  • Drawing. Lines are coloured by the magnitude of the field, with the legend's range when the surface shows the same field. Arrowheads adds small cones that show the direction, and Line thickness sets the width. Lines are trimmed to what the planes leave visible.
  • Limits. Only node vector fields are traced. Concave polyhedra are excluded (the panel reports how many), because the interpolation is only valid in convex cells. Tracing is not threaded, so a very large result may retrace visibly at each time step.

Volume rendering

Show as volume renders a field as a semi-transparent cloud instead of a surface, by ray marching through the volume. Choose the Volume field and a Volume quality (Low 32, Medium 64, High 96, Very high 128 samples along each ray: more is smoother and slower).

The opacity curve beneath the options says how opaque each value is: the horizontal axis is the normalised field value, the vertical axis the opacity. Drag a point to move it, double-click to add a point, right-click an inner point to remove it (the two end points stay at 0 and 1). Raising the curve for the hot values and lowering it for the cold ones leaves only the hot core visible; the colour ramp is the one chosen for the surface. A cell field is shown as its averaged values. The volume shows the current step.

Vector arrows

Show vector arrows draws an arrow for a vector field at sampled points of the surface: choose the Arrow field, the Arrow size (1 makes the largest arrow 5 % of the model size; the size stays stable while you zoom) and the Arrow count (about this many arrows, spread evenly over the surface). With Scale arrows by magnitude off, every arrow has the same length and only the colour shows the magnitude - useful when a few large vectors would make the rest invisible.

Stress ellipsoids (tensor glyphs)

For a symmetric stress tensor, Show stress ellipsoids draws an ellipsoid at sampled points of the surface. Its three axes point along the principal directions and their lengths follow the principal values. The longest axis is always the same size on screen, so the shape shows how the stress is distributed - a needle for a pull in one direction, a flat disc for a two-way pull, a sphere for an even pressure - and the colour shows how large it is.

  • Tensor field: every six-component field of the result whose name contains stress; the node fields come before the cell fields in the list. A tensor with another name (an OpenFOAM sigma, say) is not offered, because six components alone do not make a stress.
  • Ellipsoid size (stable while zooming) and Ellipsoid count (20 to 5000). Raise the count to 1500 or more for a plate.
  • The choices are saved with the session.

Good first tests: plate_with_hole.frd (needles along the pull far from the hole, changing sharply around it; the stress concentration is about three times the far-field stress), ibeam_cantilever.frd (bending: needles in the flanges, tilting in the web) and stress_states.vtu, a synthetic block with three tensors made to try the Tensor field choice.

Tip

The overlays combine: a deformed shape with a coloured section and a few streamlines is a perfectly good picture. Switch off what you do not use, though - the picture is clearest with one idea per view.
