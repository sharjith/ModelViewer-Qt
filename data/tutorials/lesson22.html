Lesson 22: Comparing Results, Cell and Shell Data

Introduction

Real result files differ in more than their numbers. Two analyses of the same part need to be compared fairly; element-wise data is drawn flat unless you average it; shell models have no volume to cut; and flow results arrive with units of their own. This lesson shows how ModelViewer handles each case.

Comparing Two Results

Compare shows two results of the same document side by side or stacked, with one shared camera. The example here puts bending against torsion on the same I-beam.

Step 1: Put both results in one document
Open ibeam_cantilever.frd, then use Visualization → Simulation Results → Add Result to This Document... to add ibeam_torsion.frd.
Step 2: Start the comparison
Choose the second result in Compare with: and click Compare (or use Visualization → Simulation Results → Compare Results...). Pick side by side or stacked.
Step 3: Make the colours comparable
Tick Same colour range for both: equal colours then mean equal values in both panes. Tick Link the cameras to keep both views in step when you rotate or zoom.
Step 4: Leave the comparison
Click Exit Compare (the same button) to return to a single view.

Element Data (Cell Data)

Some solvers write one value per element instead of per node. These fields carry [cells] in the Field list and every element is drawn in a single flat colour. Average cell data to nodes draws them smooth: each node takes the average of the surrounding elements, weighted by their size.

Step 1: Open the cell-data cube
Open cell_data_cube.vtk, an 8 x 8 x 8 block of hexahedra with only cell fields: a smooth Element_Stress and an integer Element_Group with four regions.
Step 2: Compare flat and averaged
Look at Element_Stress as it opens - one flat colour per element - then tick Average cell data to nodes and watch it turn smooth. Element_Group is better left flat: its regions are labels, not quantities.
Screenshot: tutorial_22_cell_data_flat_vs_smooth.png (700x350)
Element_Stress drawn flat (left) and averaged to the nodes (right)
Plot over time or line, volume rendering and iso-surfaces also work on a cell field; they always use the averaged values. A result with a single step shows its history as one dot.

Shell (Surface-Only) Results

A result made only of triangles or quads has no volume to cut. Charts still work: Plot Over Time reads the point of the surface you clicked, and Plot Over Line follows the line along the surface, leaving a gap where the line leaves it on a curved shell.

Step 1: Open the shell plate
Open shell_plate_transient.frd: a flat 2 x 1 plate of shell quads with the temperature field over 10 time steps and heat flowing in from the left edge.
Step 2: Chart it
Right-click near the middle and choose Plot Over Time Here; then try Plot Over Line... from the left edge to the right edge at the last step, and play the timeline with the chart open.

A Flow Result With Its Own Units

Open openfoam_cavity/cavity.foam (the .foam file is empty; it only points at the case). All its fields are cell data, so they are drawn flat, and the units come from the case's own dimensions: temperature in kelvin, velocity in metres per second, the symmetric tensor sigma in pascal; the kinematic pressure p has no unit. Play the five time directories (0 to 2 s) to see a vortex spin up and a temperature gradient warm.

The field values of this sample are synthetic: no OpenFOAM run produced them. They exist to exercise the reader, the cell-data display, the timeline and the units. Open your own OpenFOAM case the same way - the .foam file in the case folder.
