Lesson 20: Looking Inside a Result

Introduction

A result is drawn as its outer surface, but the interesting values are often inside. This lesson shows five ways to look in: cutting the result with a section, extracting iso-surfaces, tracing streamlines through a flow, rendering a field as a volume, and drawing vectors and stress tensors as glyphs.

You will need these samples from sample-models/Simulation: FEM_box_static_stress.vtu (sections), block.cgns (iso-surfaces and volume; block.exo and block.vtkhdf hold the same data), duct.cgns (streamlines), plate_with_hole.frd and stress_states.vtu (stress ellipsoids).

Sections

The Clipping Planes you know from Lesson 12 cut a result just like any other model, and the cut can show the field's values instead of an empty hollow.

Step 1: Open the box and a clipping plane
Open FEM_box_static_stress.vtu, click Section View in the View toolbar and enable one plane.
Step 2: Colour the cut
In the Simulation Results tab tick Colour the Clipping Plane cut with the field. The cut face now shows the stress inside the box.
Step 3: Move the plane
Change the plane's coefficient to slide the cut through the box and watch the values change.

Iso-surfaces

An iso-surface is the surface where a field has one constant value - a shell of equal temperature, say. It makes the inside of a volume visible without cutting it.

Step 1: Open a volume result
Open block.cgns, a block of hexahedra with a temperature field over five steps.
Step 2: Switch iso-surfaces on
Tick Show iso-surfaces, choose the Iso-surface field (Temperature) and the number of Iso-surface levels.
Step 3: Combine it with a section
Turn on a clipping plane as well: the nested shells are cut open and you see how the field is layered inside.
Screenshot: tutorial_20_iso_surfaces.png (700x450)
Iso-surfaces of the temperature field inside the block

Streamlines

Streamlines follow a velocity field: each line is the path a weightless particle would take if the flow were frozen at the current step.

Step 1: Open the duct
Open duct.cgns: a half-ring duct with a swirling flow over four steps.
Step 2: Show streamlines
Tick Show streamlines, choose the Streamline field (Velocity) and set the number of Streamline seeds. Arrowheads adds small cones that show the direction, and Line thickness sets the width.
Step 3: Seed on a plane
Enable a clipping plane and tick Seed on the Clipping Plane: the lines start from the cut, which shows exactly where the flow goes from that cross-section.
Screenshot: tutorial_20_streamlines.png (700x450)
Streamlines swirling through the duct, with arrowheads

Volume Rendering

Show as volume renders a field as a semi-transparent cloud instead of a surface. Choose the Volume field, a Volume quality (Low 32 up to Very high 128) and shape the opacity curve so that only the range of values you care about is visible.

Step 1: Use the block again
With block.cgns open, switch off the iso-surfaces and tick Show as volume with Temperature as the volume field.
Step 2: Shape the opacity
Edit the opacity curve: drag a point, double-click to add one, right-click an inner point to remove it. Raise the curve for the hot values and lower it for the cold ones to see only the hot core.
Step 3: Step through time
Press play on the timeline: the cloud follows the five steps of the result.
Screenshot: tutorial_20_volume_rendering.png (700x450)
The temperature field of the block rendered as a volume

Vector Arrows

Show vector arrows draws an arrow for a vector field at sampled points of the surface. Choose the Arrow field, the Arrow size (stable while you zoom) and the Arrow count. With Scale arrows by magnitude off, every arrow has the same length and only its colour shows the magnitude.

Stress Ellipsoids

For a symmetric stress tensor, Show stress ellipsoids draws an ellipsoid at sampled points. Its three axes point along the principal directions and their lengths follow the principal values; the longest axis is always the same size on screen, so the shape shows how the stress is distributed and the colour shows how large it is.

Step 1: Start with the plate
Open plate_with_hole.frd, tick Show stress ellipsoids and set Ellipsoid count to 1500 or more. Far from the hole the ellipsoids are needles lying along the pull; around the hole they change shape and colour sharply.
Step 2: Choose between tensors
Open stress_states.vtu, which has three stress tensors, and switch the Tensor field: needles for bending, tilted discs for torsion, flattened ellipsoids for the element-wise tensor.
