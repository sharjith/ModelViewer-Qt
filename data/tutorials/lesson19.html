Lesson 19: Simulation Results

Introduction

ModelViewer shows the results that a solver has already written - it does not run simulations. Open the result file of a finite-element or flow solver and the model appears as its outer surface, coloured by a result field such as stress, temperature or velocity. This lesson covers opening a result, choosing what to colour by, showing the deformation, setting units and stepping through time.

Supported formats: VTK (.vtk, .vtu), CalculiX .frd, Exodus II, CGNS, MED, OpenFOAM (open the .foam file) and VTKHDF. Exodus, CGNS, VTKHDF and MED need a build that includes the matching library - if an extension is missing from the Open dialog, your build was made without it.

Sample files for this lesson

All of them are in the sample-models/Simulation folder of the installation.

FileWhat it is
FEM_box_static_stress.vtuA static analysis of a 10 mm box. Opens coloured by von Mises stress.
FEM_box_load_steps.frdThe same box, with the load ramped over four steps.
FEM_box_modes.frdThe first six vibration modes, with their frequencies.
FEM_box_thermal_transient.frdHeat flowing into the box over 20 time steps.
plate_with_hole.frdA plate with a hole, pulled in one direction: the stress concentration at the hole.

Opening a Result

Step 1: Open the file
Choose File → Open... and pick FEM_box_static_stress.vtu. A result opens in its own document.
Step 2: Look at the surface
The box appears coloured by von Mises stress, with a colour legend. Only the outer surface is drawn; Lesson 20 shows how to look inside.
Step 3: Open the Simulation Results tab
The tab sits in the bottom-left dock, next to Selections and States. All the controls in this lesson are there.

Choosing What to Colour By

  • Field and Component: the field to colour by (for example Displacement, von Mises, Tresca or a principal stress) and one component of it or its magnitude. Fields marked [cells] hold one value per element and are drawn flat.
  • Range: Automatic (all steps) keeps the colours comparable between steps, Automatic (this step) rescales every step, and Custom lets you type a minimum and a maximum.
  • Colormap and Contours: change the colour ramp, or turn the smooth gradient into a set of discrete bands.

Deformation and Extremes

Step 1: Open plate_with_hole.frd
Open it from the same folder. It has four load steps and is coloured by von Mises stress.
Step 2: Show the deformed shape
Tick Show deformed shape. The model moves by its displacement field; Scale factor exaggerates the movement, and Auto picks a factor that makes it visible.
Step 3: Mark the extremes
Tick Mark minimum and maximum. The lowest and highest values are labelled on the visible surface - for this plate, the maximum sits at the side of the hole.
Screenshot: tutorial_19_deformed_plate.png (700x450)
plate_with_hole.frd deformed, with the minimum and maximum marked
The deformation is exaggerated on purpose. Keep the scale factor in mind when you judge how much a part really moves.

Units

Many result files do not say what unit their values are in. Quantity and Values are in let you state it - for example a temperature in degrees Celsius or kelvin, a stress in megapascals - and the legend then shows the unit. FEM_box_thermal_transient.frd states no temperature unit, so none is assumed until you choose one.

Stepping Through Time

A result with several steps - load steps, vibration modes or time steps - shows a timeline at the top of the viewport with play/pause, previous and next step, stop, a slider, Loop and a speed choice.

Step 1: Play the load steps
Open FEM_box_load_steps.frd and press play. The colour range covers all steps by default, so a colour means the same value in every frame and you can watch the stress grow.
Step 2: Step through the vibration modes
Open FEM_box_modes.frd. Each step is a mode, labelled Mode 1 to 6 with its frequency in hertz. Turn on Show deformed shape and step through them.
Step 3: Watch the heat flow in
Open FEM_box_thermal_transient.frd: one face is held at 100, the rest starts at 20, and the heat spreads over 20 time steps.

Adding a Result to the Current Document

File → Open puts a result in a document of its own. To see a result together with other models - say a CAD part and its analysis - use Visualization → Simulation Results → Add Result to This Document.... The Result box in the tab chooses which result the controls act on.

Saving

A result is stored inside a .mvf session together with its view settings: field, range, colormap, deformation, sections and more. Reopening the session brings the view back as you left it.

Results are drawn flat in every rendering mode and are not drawn by the ray tracer, so PBR and ray tracing add little for them.

Try It Yourself

  • Open plate_with_hole.frd, play the four load steps with Show deformed shape on, and find the hottest spot.
  • Switch Field to a principal stress and compare it with von Mises; then try Custom under Range.
  • Open FEM_box_thermal_transient.frd and choose a temperature unit under Values are in; watch the legend change.
  • Save the document as .mvf, close it and open it again.
