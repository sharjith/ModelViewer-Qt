Lesson 27: Measure, Annotate and Report

Introduction

ModelViewer measures directly on the model: points, distances, angles, radii, diameters, areas, edge lengths and the distance along a surface. You can leave sticky-note annotations on the model and put it all into a PDF report with captured views. This lesson takes the tools in order of how often you will use them.

Practice on bottle.step, CAD.step or the Forklift: STEP, IGES and BREP models carry exact edges and surfaces, so the radius and concentricity tools can snap to analytic circle centres. On OBJ and glTF meshes the same tools use the mesh geometry and some of them are not available.

Your First Measurement

Step 1: Open the Measure dialog
Choose Tools → Measure.... There is no toolbar button or keyboard shortcut for it.
Step 2: Pick a tool and click
Choose Distance in Tool: and click two points on the model. The result appears in the Measurements: list and as a label with a dimension line on the model.
Step 3: Keep going or finish
The tool stays armed for the next measurement. For tools that take any number of picks (Pitch Circle, Chain Length) press Enter or click Finish. Select a measurement in the list to find it on the model, and Delete removes it.
Screenshot: tutorial_27_distance_measurement.png (700x450)
A distance measurement between two points on a STEP model, with the Measure dialog open
Creating, deleting and moving a measurement or annotation can be undone; the visibility checkbox in the list cannot (like mesh visibility in the Scene Tree). A measurement is resolved live against the mesh, so it stays correct if you move or transform the mesh afterwards.

The Tools

The tools are grouped as Point & Distance, Arcs & Circles, Faces and Edges.

ToolPicksWhat it measures
Point1 clickThe 3D coordinates of one point
Distance2 clicksThe straight-line distance between two points, which may lie on different meshes
Geodesic Distance2 clicksThe distance measured ALONG the surface, for example around a curved part; both points must be on the same mesh
3-Point Angle3 clicksThe angle at the first picked vertex between the rays to the other two points
ToolPicksWhat it measures
3-Point Arc Radius3 clicksRadius and centre of an arc fitted through three points on its rim
Center + 2-Point Arc Radius3 clicksRadius of an arc or hole from a picked centre plus two rim points; on STEP/IGES/BREP the centre snaps to the exact centre, even for a through hole
Edge Radius1 clickExact radius, centre and axis of a circular edge (a hole or boss rim); STEP/IGES/BREP only
Pitch Circle3+ clicks, then Enter/FinishDiameter of the best-fit circle through three or more hole centres (a bolt-hole pattern) and the angle between neighbouring holes
Concentricity2 clicksWhether two holes or bosses share one axis: distance between centres and angle between axes; STEP/IGES/BREP only
Cylindrical/Conical Diameter1 click on the curved surface, not its rimDiameter of a cylinder or cone at the picked point (it varies along a cone); has its own options below
ToolPicksWhat it measures
Face to Face2 clicksPerpendicular distance between two near-parallel faces, otherwise the angle between them
Point to Face2 clicksPerpendicular distance from a point to the plane of a face
Face Area1 clickArea of a face: the picked triangle plus every connected, coplanar triangle
Minimum Distance2 clicksTrue closest-point distance between two surfaces; each pick expands to its whole smooth region. Works on one mesh too, for example a wall-thickness check
ToolPicksWhat it measures
Edge Length1 clickLength of one edge, straight or curved
Edge to Vertex2 clicksPerpendicular distance from a point to the line of an edge
Edge to Edge2 clicksPerpendicular distance between two near-parallel edges, otherwise the angle; also handles skew edges (angle only)
Edge to Face2 clicksPerpendicular distance from an edge to the plane of a face, otherwise the angle
Chain Length2+ clicks, then Enter/FinishTotal length of a connected run of edges - a weld seam or a closed perimeter; each new edge must share an endpoint with the chain so far

Cylindrical Diameter Options

While Cylindrical/Conical Diameter is the active tool, extra options tune the fit used on non-CAD meshes (they are session-only, not saved with the document): Angle tolerance (35 degrees by default), Min region size (24 points) and Min / Max diameter (0 = no limit) to reject fits that are too small or too large.

Annotations

An annotation is a free-text sticky note anchored to a point on a mesh surface and joined to a draggable text label by a leader line. Open it with Tools → Annotate.... Measure and Annotate are mutually exclusive: arming one disarms the other.

Step 1: Place a note
The dialog opens unarmed, which is useful for reviewing existing notes. Click Place Note, then click a point on the model. The note appears with the default text "New Note" selected, so you can type over it at once; the tool stays armed for more notes.
Step 2: Edit, move, delete
Select a note in the list (or click it in the viewport) to edit its text or Delete it; a multi-select delete is one undo step. Drag a note's text frame to move the label and leader independently of the anchor point.

Export Report

Tools → Export Report... writes a PDF made from captured camera views and, optionally, a table of every measurement and annotation.

Step 1: Capture the views
Set up each view you want in the report and click Capture View in the Cameras panel. Captured views appear in the report dialog automatically.
Step 2: Choose what each view shows
Double-click a captured view to choose which measurements and annotations that view's picture shows, independent of their visibility in the document. Use Current Visibility clears the override.
Step 3: Include the table and export
Tick Include measurement/annotation table to append a table of all measurements and annotations, then export. When it finishes, the document's real visibility and the viewport camera are restored exactly as they were.

Measurements and annotations are saved only in the native .mvf session format. They are not exported to glTF or GLB, which have no concept of them - include them in a report instead.
